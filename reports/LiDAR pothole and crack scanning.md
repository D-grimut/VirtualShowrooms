# Split the scan: LiDAR for depth, pixels for cracks

Build two capture modes on one ARKit session rather than replacing the mesh export with a heavier reconstruction. For **potholes**, skip `ARMeshAnchor` and fuse ARKit's raw `sceneDepth` (256×192 depth plus confidence, every frame) into a small heightmap aligned to the pavement plane, at 2–5 mm resolution. Fit the reference surface on the intact ring around the hole, and integrate depth below that surface to get area, volume and a high-percentile maximum depth. For **cracks**, accept that LiDAR cannot see them and measure them in 12 MP `captureHighResolutionFrame` stills with an on-device Core ML segmentation model. Convert pixels to millimetres by casting rays from the high-res camera onto the LiDAR-fitted plane. The published evidence supports roughly **3–10 mm depth error and 5–10% volume error** for a close, short, single-pothole scan, and roughly **±0.2–0.5 mm crack-width error on cracks of 0.5 mm or wider** at about 0.3 m. The crack figure comes from comparable smartphone studies plus pixel arithmetic; no published study has tested this exact pipeline. Both modes use a few megabytes of working memory and run in seconds, compared with the ~4 GB free-storage check reported for Object Capture. The honest limit is that pothole depth sits close to the sensor's noise floor near the 13 mm and 25 mm severity boundaries, and crack depth is not measured at all. The app should state both openly.

## The sensor, not the mesh, sets the ceiling

The coarse OBJ is a symptom, not the root cause. ARKit's depth map is a **256×192 float32 buffer in metres with a three-level confidence map**, which Apple itself calls "precise, low-resolution depth" ([Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/displaying-a-point-cloud-using-scene-depth)). On iPad Pro it is not raw time-of-flight but a product that "fuses LiDAR data with color data through a machine learning algorithm" ([Zea & Hanebeck, JAIF 2022](https://isif.org/media/66)). `ARMeshAnchor` is a real-time, room-scale mesh built for occlusion and physics from that same stream. Apple documents no vertex spacing for it, so measure its mean edge length on a flat floor before deciding anything. The researchers who evaluate iPhone scanning accuracy test apps that export points from raw depth, not the scene mesh ([Teppati Losè et al. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/17/4157)). Even raw depth has hard limits. Independent work puts iPhone LiDAR at **±1 cm absolute for objects larger than 10 cm**, with precision falling below that size ([Luetzenburg et al. 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8593014/)), and at about 1–3 cm across typical survey scans ([Poliba 2022](https://iris.poliba.it/retrieve/7da9a53f-82ec-4471-9402-88aec7532fe7/2022_Smartphone_LiDAR_Technologies_for_Surveying_pdfeditoriale.pdf)). The quoted ranging spec is ±1–2% out to about 5 m ([IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/node/15492)).

Distance turns these numbers into a working envelope. With the depth-scaled focal length of roughly 195–215 px, one depth pixel covers about **1.5 mm at 0.3 m, 2.6 mm at 0.5 m and 5 mm at 1.0 m**. The true independent resolution is coarser, because the map is densified from a sparse emitter. A ±1–2% range error means 5–10 mm of absolute error at 0.5 m. These figures are calculations from typical intrinsics; read `frame.camera.intrinsics` on the device. Pothole depth is a relative measurement against the surrounding pavement in the same scan, so much of that absolute bias cancels out. Cracks are a different matter. A 1–3 mm crack is narrower than one depth pixel even at 0.3 m, and the ML densification smooths it away. A Mineta Transportation Institute field study confirmed this against a Leica P20 laser scanner: iPad and iPhone scans "could not detect smaller cracks and shallow potholes", and "vertical deformations in surfaces less than 3-4 cm were not identifiable" on area-scale walking scans ([ROSA P](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/75721); [MTI brief](https://transweb.sjsu.edu/sites/default/files/2224-RB-Peterson-LiDAR-Surface-Texture-Assessment.pdf)).

The alternatives do not escape this ceiling cheaply. On iOS, `PhotogrammetrySession` supports only the `.reduced` detail level; medium, full and raw are macOS-only ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/photogrammetrysession/request/detail.md)). The ~4 GB figure appears in an `insufficientStorage` log line ("required 4000000000") rather than in Apple documentation ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/729249)). On-device Gaussian splatting, as in Scaniverse, looks good but is "harder to convert into a mesh" and harder to measure ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/scaniverse-introduces-gaussian-splatting)). The road-inspection products also point away from 3D crack geometry: Vialytics and RoadBotics classify cracks from 2D phone imagery with cloud AI ([Traffic Technology Today](https://www.traffictechnologytoday.com/?p=13789); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/cars-that-think/transportation/sensors/roadbotics-ai-could-change-the-way-cities-maintain-roads)). The right design therefore gives each defect its own sensor: depth for holes and pixels for cracks.

## Potholes: fuse raw depth into a 2 mm plane-aligned heightmap

The quantification algorithm is well established across Kinect, RealSense, terrestrial-laser and iPhone studies. Fit a reference surface to the intact pavement, compute signed depth below it, segment the hole by a depth threshold, then take the area from the footprint and the volume by integrating depth over grid cells or TIN prisms ([US 9196048](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9196048); [arXiv 2505.04207](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04207v1); [Hindawi](https://ideas.repec.org/a/hin/jnlmpe/9157849.html)). Your contribution is doing this on fused raw depth instead of on the mesh. Since pavement and floors are 2.5D, a full 3D TSDF is unnecessary. Fit the plane first, then accumulate each high-confidence depth sample into a heightmap grid in plane coordinates, using a confidence-weighted running mean. At 2 mm cells over 1 × 1 m, that is 250,000 cells. Storing a weighted sum, a weight and a sum of squares in float32 needs about 3 MB, compared with an estimated 25–200 MB for a dense 3D TSDF at 4–2 mm. The 49k depth pixels per frame are a trivial GPU load at frame rate. Apple's point-cloud sample already provides most of the Metal pipeline: wrapping depth as an `r32Float` texture, unprojecting with intrinsics, filtering by confidence and colouring from `capturedImage` ([Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/displaying-a-point-cloud-using-scene-depth)). No maintained Swift or Metal TSDF library turned up in the research, so writing your own heightmap kernel is both the lighter and the more available path.

Three pitfalls in the literature shape the fit. RANSAC degrades when the hole fills much of the crop, because its reliability depends on the ratio of plane points to total points ([Gdańsk Univ. of Technology](https://pub.pg.edu.pl/en/publication/140400/applying-ransac-algorithm-for-fitting-scanning-strips-from-airborne-laser-scanning)). Fit on a ring of intact surface about 0.3–0.5 m beyond the visible rim, with a 5–10 mm inlier tolerance, then refine by least squares. Crowned roads need a quadratic surface, an approach that fits a parabolic arch per cross-section ([ASCE CICTP 2021](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/9780784483565.045)). One 2025 study found a cloth-simulation datum beat RANSAC for pothole extraction precision, **87.5% against 66.0%** ([Laser & Optoelectronics Progress 2025](https://www.researching.cn/articles/OJ7d2cc5e37e86884d/figureandtable)). Ship plane-plus-optional-quadratic first and keep the datum swappable. Stones show up as negative depth and should be clipped to zero ([Aston](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/performance-assessment-of-kinect-as-a-sensor-for-pothole-imaging-/)). Wet, glossy or dark surfaces degrade iPhone LiDAR returns ([PLOS ONE 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0344604)), so standing water should block measurement rather than produce a number.

Accuracy expectations come from a small set of studies. The iRodd smartphone-LiDAR study reports **5.35% average volume error and 6.47 mm RMSE** ([JIPD 2024](https://systems.enpress-publisher.com/index.php/jipd/article/view/6162)). A TUM handheld study measured **3.5–4.4% planimetric error but 10.6% depth error**, with depth consistently overestimated ([TUM](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/a-mobile-mapping-solution-for-vru-infrastructure-monitoring-via-l/)). Kinect v2 reached 2.58% depth and 5.47% volume error ([Aston](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/performance-assessment-of-kinect-as-a-sensor-for-pothole-imaging-/)). The negative MTI result came from long walking scans processed in a third-party app, where "longer scans … led to geometric anomalies" ([ROSA P](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/75721)). Keep each scan to one hole, about 10–30 s, held slowly over the hole. Multi-frame fusion should push random noise below the single-frame level. A fused relative accuracy of 2–5 mm on a smooth patch is plausible but unmeasured: correlated pixels, near-static poses and range bias all resist averaging, so vary the viewpoint during the sweep.

These numbers matter because of the severity bands. PCI (ASTM D6433) grades potholes by maximum depth bands of **13–25, 25–50 and over 50 mm** crossed with diameter bands of 100–200, 200–450 and 450–750 mm ([TotalPave PCI training](https://totalpave.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/PCI-Asphalt-Distress-Training.pdf); [FAA PAVEAIR](https://faapaveair.faa.gov/Help/Documents/levelsofseverityforpotholes.htm)). The 13 mm floor is roughly the sensor's noise floor, so use it as the segmentation threshold. Report any depth within about 5–10 mm of a band boundary as "borderline", not as a confident grade. The exact L/M/H cell matrix in the notes is reconstructed from general knowledge, so verify it against ASTM D6433 before shipping. Diameter error of 3–5% is comfortably inside PCI's 10% linear and 20% area extent tolerances. One more caution for users comparing against tape measurements: a manual cuboid L × W × D overstates pothole volume by **2–4×** relative to a TIN integral ([Civil Engineering Journal 2025](https://civilejournal.org/index.php/cej/article/view/5954)), so validate against sand or water fill instead.

## Cracks: segment 12 MP stills, use LiDAR only for scale

The RGB camera resolves cracks that the depth sensor cannot. Since iOS 16, `captureHighResolutionFrame` returns an `ARFrame` with the full 12 MP wide-camera image "and all other frame properties", captured out of band while tracking continues. It requires `recommendedVideoFormatForHighResolutionFrameCapturing` ([WWDC22 10126](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10126/); [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arsession/capturehighresolutionframe(completion:))). At 4032 px with a focal length of roughly 3,030 px, the image samples about **0.10 mm/px at 0.3 m and 0.165 mm/px at 0.5 m**, compared with about 1.5–2.6 mm for depth. These are calculations; read the real intrinsics on the device. The scale step is the key design choice. Do not rely on per-pixel depth for the high-res frame, which is unconfirmed and in any case only 256×192. Instead, fit the floor or pavement plane in world space from confidence-filtered LiDAR points gathered over several regular frames. Then intersect each mask pixel's ray, built from the high-res frame's intrinsics and `camera.transform`, with that plane. This removes tilt and perspective errors that a single mm-per-pixel factor would introduce. It also makes the scale error roughly equal to the relative plane error, which averages down over thousands of points to well under 1%. Width error is then dominated by pixel quantisation and the segmentation boundary, not by LiDAR.

For segmentation, the fastest prototype is a YOLO11n-seg or YOLO26n-seg model fine-tuned on the public-domain Ultralytics Crack-seg dataset (4,029 images) and exported with `format=coreml` ([Ultralytics Crack-seg](https://docs.ultralytics.com/datasets/segment/crack-seg/)). Ultralytics measured YOLO26n segmentation at **4.8 ms per 640 image on an iPhone 17 Pro with the Neural Engine** ([Ultralytics CoreML](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/coreml/)). Two caveats drive the production choice. First, Ultralytics code and weights are **AGPL-3.0** ([HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/spaces/imesper/FastSAM/blob/main/ultralytics/yolo/v8/__init__.py)), so a closed-source app needs an Enterprise licence or a permissive model. Apache-2.0 HrSegNet with CrackSeg9k weights is one ready option ([HrSegNet](https://github.com/CHDyshli/HrSegNet4CrackSegmentation)). Second, YOLO-seg prototype masks come out at roughly a quarter of the input resolution, which is too coarse for sub-millimetre width. The architecture below therefore treats YOLO or a semantic model as region-of-interest finding. It tiles the 12 MP still into 512–640 px crops and refines the edges with classical sub-pixel methods. Dataset licences matter separately: DeepCrack is restricted to non-commercial use ([yhlleo/DeepCrack](https://github.com/yhlleo/DeepCrack)), and the research found no public model for indoor floor cracks in tile, screed or epoxy. Budget for collecting and labelling your own floor images. Cross-dataset generalisation drops sharply, with some baselines falling to 30–44% in zero-shot tests ([FlexiCrackNet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18855)).

Measurement follows a standard recipe. Skeletonise the mask, take width as twice the Euclidean distance transform at skeleton pixels, and take length as the summed skeleton arc length ([MATLAB Central](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/answers/223717-crack-width-measurement-on-binary-images)). Orthogonal edge-to-edge measurement reduces overestimation at branches and oblique cracks ([MDPI Buildings 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/15/14/2489); [arXiv 2511.02144](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02144)). Sub-pixel edge methods achieved **3–9% relative width error and under 0.30 mm length error** ([Buildings 2024](https://doaj.org/article/0e893c5646d74c7da7e60e62133afeb3)), and cracks under 5 px wide need such methods ([US 11551341](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11551341)). Comparable smartphone systems land in the same range. An Android app validated against a calibration plate showed deviations mostly between **−0.5 and +0.6 mm** ([Airiti thesis](https://www.airitilibrary.com/Article/Detail/U0020-2108202221494100)). A laser-spot-scaled method reached 0.02–0.57 mm absolute error ([Applied Sciences 2023](https://doaj.org/article/30b8e36351044dbdbf2aac434e9b6ad8)). A camera-plus-LiDAR research rig claims under 0.1 mm mean absolute error but under 15% relative error, with the worst errors on the narrowest cracks ([arXiv 2501.09203](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09203)). Requiring at least three pixels across a crack puts the minimum reliably measurable width at about **0.3 mm from a 12 MP still at 0.3 m**. Narrower hairlines can be detected but should be reported as "under 0.3 mm", not given a number. That resolution is far finer than the severity thresholds that matter: FHWA uses 3 and 6 mm for concrete and 6 and 19 mm for asphalt, and PCI uses 10 and 75 mm ([FHWA DIM](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/infrastructure/pavements/ltpp/13092/13092.pdf); [TotalPave](https://totalpave.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/PCI-Asphalt-Distress-Training.pdf)).

Crack length is where errors grow. Within one still, length is accurate to within a few pixels. Long cracks need several stills projected into a shared plane-aligned orthomosaic, for example at 0.1 mm per cell, and fused there. ARKit pose drift then dominates: a developer reports "several-centimetre discrepancies" over longer walks ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/842466)), and a smartphone-LiDAR study found crack-length RMSE of 1.7–2.2 cm against TLS ([UiTM](https://update.library.uitm.edu.my/Record/2-s2.0-85203788019/Details)). Keep multi-shot crack runs short, overlap the stills, and add image-feature registration between them if lengths beyond about a metre matter.

## Implementation outline for iOS 18 in Swift

Run a single `ARWorldTrackingConfiguration` for both modes, so the plane, poses and stills share one world frame.

```swift
let config = ARWorldTrackingConfiguration()
config.frameSemantics = [.sceneDepth, .smoothedSceneDepth]
config.planeDetection = [.horizontal]            // cross-check / fallback only
if let fmt = ARWorldTrackingConfiguration.recommendedVideoFormatForHighResolutionFrameCapturing {
    config.videoFormat = fmt                     // required for 12 MP stills
}
// Do NOT enable .sceneReconstruction for measurement; keep it only if you still want the preview mesh.
session.run(config)
```

The stages below are the build order. Each one is testable on its own.

| Stage | Pothole mode | Crack mode | Key APIs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Plane | Accumulate `.high`-confidence depth points (confidence raw value 2) from about 10 frames. RANSAC on a ring outside the hole, 5–10 mm tolerance, least-squares refine, optional quadratic. | Same plane fit on the floor area in view. | `ARFrame.sceneDepth.depthMap` / `.confidenceMap`, `camera.intrinsics` scaled to 256×192, `camera.transform` |
| 2. Capture | 10–30 s slow sweep about 0.4–0.7 m above the hole, mostly near-nadir plus a few 30–45° oblique passes to see the walls. | Hold at about 0.3 m, fronto-parallel. Trigger 12 MP stills, one in flight at a time. | `ARSession.captureHighResolutionFrame` (async), handle `highResolutionFrameCaptureInProgress` |
| 3. Fuse / segment | Metal compute kernel: unproject each depth pixel, transform to plane coordinates, add to a 2 mm heightmap with weight = confidence × cos(incidence) / d². Reject pixels near depth discontinuities. | Tile the still into 512–640 px crops and run the Core ML segmenter on the Neural Engine. | `MTLComputePipelineState`, `CVMetalTextureCache`, `VNCoreMLRequest` or `MLModel` with `.all` compute units |
| 4. Measure | Signed depth = plane minus height. Cells deeper than 13 mm form connected components. Area = cell count × cell area. Volume = Σ depth × cell area. Max depth = 98th–99th percentile. Diameter = mean of the longest and perpendicular extents. | Skeletonise (Zhang-Suen). For each skeleton point, find sub-pixel edges along the normal, cast both edge rays onto the plane, and take the 3D distance as width. Length = Σ 3D skeleton steps. | `MPSImageEuclideanDistanceTransform`, vImage/Core Image morphology, `simd` ray-plane math |
| 5. Report | Depth ± band, area, volume, PCI grade with a "borderline" flag. Block the result when water or glare drives low-confidence cells. | Max, mean and P95 width. Length. FHWA/PCI class. "Under 0.3 mm" hairline flag. | |
| 6. Export | Heightmap as a regular-grid mesh (two triangles per cell) textured with an orthophoto, plus a JSON of the measurements. Far finer than the scene mesh where it matters. | Orthomosaic PNG with the mask overlay, plus JSON of the polylines with widths. | `ModelIO` / OBJ writer |

A few details avoid weeks of debugging. ARKit intrinsics refer to the captured-image resolution, so scale fx, fy, cx and cy by 256/W before unprojecting depth ([Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/displaying-a-point-cloud-using-scene-depth)). Verify on the device whether the high-res frame's intrinsics already match the 12 MP image, and rescale by `capturedImage` size over `camera.imageResolution` if not. Measure the actual depth frame rate from `ARFrame.timestamp` deltas: one paper pairs 256×192 depth with 30 fps RGB ([arXiv 2310.14437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14437)), while 60 Hz is commonly quoted but unconfirmed. Use raw `sceneDepth` for fusion, because your own temporal averaging replaces ARKit's smoothing and avoids double-filtering. Use `smoothedSceneDepth` for the live preview. Apple staff advise tearing down capture UI before heavy processing ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/761841)). That matters little here, because both pipelines finish in well under a second of GPU and Neural Engine time per still, by extrapolation from the 4.8 ms tile benchmark. Benchmark on the oldest device you support, such as A14. Do not try to recover crack depth with guided depth upsampling: Apple describes its own `MPSImageGuidedFilter` upsampling as creating "the illusion of more depth information" ([Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/displaying-a-point-cloud-using-scene-depth)). It cleans pothole rims for display and adds no measurable detail.

Keep an optional offload path in reserve rather than in the first release. The industry pattern for finer geometry is cloud or Mac processing: Polycam Photo, KIRI, Scaniverse business accounts and Apple's macOS-only detail levels all work this way ([KIRI FAQ](https://www.kiriengine.app/faq/how-does-kiri-engine-process-the-files); [Scaniverse release notes](https://www.nianticspatial.com/capture/scaniverse-release-notes)). Because you already hold ARKit poses, depth and 12 MP stills, a later "precision upload" could feed them as pose priors to COLMAP-style triangulation ([Colmap-PCD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05504); [arXiv 2303.15060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.15060)). No source shows that this reaches millimetre accuracy on pavement, so treat it as an experiment.

## What the app measures, what it infers, and how to prove it

Be explicit in the UI and the export about which numbers are observations and which are model output. **Measured:** pothole depth below the fitted reference surface, at every heightmap cell that received high-confidence returns. Pothole footprint and area, from cells crossing the threshold. Crack width and length in image pixels, converted to millimetres by a plane that LiDAR measured. **Derived from measurements:** volume, which integrates measured cells but has to interpolate cells under overhanging rims or in steep-wall shadow that the sensor never saw. Report the fraction of the footprint that is interpolated. PCI and FHWA severity grades, which apply thresholds to noisy inputs and therefore need the "borderline" flag. **Inferred by models:** the crack mask boundary, which is a neural network's judgement and is the largest single source of width error. The reference pavement surface, which is an assumption about what the intact road looked like; crowned or warped surfaces bias it. **Not measured at all:** crack depth, which neither the LiDAR footprint nor a single image resolves, and anything under standing water. The headline accuracy figures also carry different weights. The pothole volume and depth errors come from peer-reviewed smartphone studies. The fused 2–5 mm relative depth estimate and the ±0.2–0.5 mm crack-width estimate are engineering projections from comparable systems and pixel arithmetic. No study has validated the `captureHighResolutionFrame` plus plane-intersection pipeline.

That gap makes a validation harness part of the architecture, not an afterthought. For potholes, scan sand- or water-filled reference holes, or 3D-printed or cast depressions of known volume, at 13, 25 and 50 mm depths, and record bias and spread per distance. Since depth tends to be overestimated ([TUM](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/a-mobile-mapping-solution-for-vru-infrastructure-monitoring-via-l/)), you can correct a measured bias on each device. For cracks, photograph a printed crack-width comparator card or calibration plate at 0.25, 0.3 and 0.4 m, as the Taiwanese app study did ([Airiti thesis](https://www.airitilibrary.com/Article/Detail/U0020-2108202221494100)). Accept the published numbers only after your harness reproduces them on your target devices. An in-frame scale target is also worth offering as an optional "survey mode". Referenced mobile-LiDAR crack scans reached about ±0.5 mm while unreferenced ones exceeded 1.5 mm ([Erciyes](https://avesis.erciyes.edu.tr/publication/details/f02a9c53-16cf-4f97-8c6c-fcaa8f8e6632/oai)), which suggests a cheap reference card buys real accuracy.

## Conclusion

The way past the coarse mesh is to stop asking one model to carry two kinds of measurement, not to build a more detailed one. The two defects sit on opposite sides of the sensor's resolution limit. Potholes are centimetre-scale relief that LiDAR resolves once you fuse the raw depth yourself. Cracks are sub-millimetre contrast features that only the camera resolves, and LiDAR's job there shrinks to supplying a metric plane. Building around that split makes the app lighter and more accurate than Object Capture at once. The output also changes shape: a measurement record with uncertainty attached, of which the 3D model is only one part.

The uncomfortable implication is that pothole depth, the number users care about most, is the weakest measurement. It sits within a centimetre of the bands that decide severity. A product that reports "28 mm ± 8, borderline M/H" will earn more trust from road engineers than one that reports "28 mm". The calibration harness, the device-specific bias correction and the confidence flags are therefore what separate a credible measuring tool from a scanning toy.
